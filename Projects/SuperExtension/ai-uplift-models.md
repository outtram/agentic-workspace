# AI Uplift Models

**Baseline:** 10-person squad · ~2,000 story points / year

---

## 1. AI Integration Depth

How aggressively do we embed AI into the delivery model?

### Option A — Embed & Learn

One AI-enabled engineer (Nithin) replaces a traditional dev role in Squad 2. Builds AI tooling *while delivering* against the existing backlog.

| | Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Us (Consulting)** | Low investment risk. Proves the concept with real delivery metrics. Limited upside — we're selling one seat, not a model. Nithin is stretched between delivery and tooling. |
| **Client** | Minimal disruption to existing squad. Easy to approve. Hard to see transformational ROI — looks like a headcount swap. No dependency on new ways of working. |

### Option B — Hybrid Squad

Blend of AI-enabled and traditional roles. Gradually replace manual effort with agentic tooling as it matures. E.g. 7 traditional + 3 AI-enabled.

| | Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Us (Consulting)** | Balanced risk. Gives time to build & iterate tooling while still delivering. Creates a migration path to Option C. Harder to define commercially — what exactly are we selling? |
| **Client** | Feels progressive but safe. Velocity should hold or improve. Requires some change management. Benefits are incremental, not headline-grabbing. |

### Option C — All-In AI-First Squad

Dedicated 5-person AI-native squad. Purpose-built to maximise throughput using agentic engineering. Targets Squad 2 scope but tooling can be reused across Squads 1 and 3.

| | Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Us (Consulting)** | Highest upside — we're selling a *capability*, not headcount. Reusable IP across other clients. Higher delivery risk if tooling underperforms. Need strong proof points early. |
| **Client** | Potentially transformational. 5 people doing the work of 10 is a compelling story. But high trust required — unproven model. What happens if it fails mid-sprint? Compound benefit: tooling built here flows to other squads over time. |

## 2. Commercial Model

How is the engagement priced and who carries the risk?

### Model A — Fixed Cost (We Absorb Risk)

Price at the same rate as the current Squad 2. We deliver using fewer/different people, absorb the cost of AI tooling investment, and keep the margin upside.

| | Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Us (Consulting)** | Full control over how we deliver. If AI tooling works, margins expand significantly. If it doesn't, we eat the cost. Clean commercial story — client pays the same. |
| **Client** | Zero risk. Same cost, same (or better) output. Easy to approve internally. But no skin in the game — less incentive to champion or protect the engagement. |

### Model B — 50/50 Shared Investment

Split the cost of AI uplift. Savings from efficiency gains (e.g. replacing 4 testers with 2 AI-enabled testers) are shared 50/50.

| | Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Us (Consulting)** | Reduces our upfront investment risk. Aligns incentives — both parties win when AI delivers. Requires transparent metrics to prove savings. More complex commercial negotiation. |
| **Client** | Co-investment signals partnership, not just vendor relationship. Gets AI capability at half the cost. But requires internal budget approval for something unproven. Savings must be measurable and auditable. |

### Model C — Client-Funded Uplift

Client pays separately for the AI tooling build (Agents, Skills, automation). They own the output uplift directly.

| | Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Us (Consulting)** | Funded R&D — lowest financial risk for us. But client owns the IP narrative. Harder to reuse tooling across other clients. Uplift of 5–10% may feel underwhelming vs. the investment. |
| **Client** | Full ownership of the AI capability. Can apply it beyond our engagement. But paying for uncertain returns. Internal stakeholders will ask: "why are we funding a consultancy's R&D?" |

## 3. Value Realisation & Risk

How do we prove it works and protect both sides?

### Key Questions

- **How do we measure success?** Story points alone aren't enough. Need quality, cycle time, defect rates, and rework metrics.
- **What's the minimum viable proof?** A 4–6 week pilot with clear before/after metrics on a contained scope.
- **What if it underperforms?** Need a rollback clause or hybrid fallback so the client isn't left exposed.
- **IP ownership?** Who owns the Agents/Skills built during the engagement? Critical for both sides.
- **Change management?** AI-first delivery changes how the squad works day-to-day. Existing team members may resist or feel threatened.

### Risk Matrix

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| AI tooling underdelivers | Velocity drops, client loses confidence | Pilot period with clear exit criteria |
| Team resistance / skill gap | Slow adoption, shadow processes | Upskilling plan, pair with AI-enabled engineers |
| Commercial complexity | Deal stalls in procurement | Start with Model A (fixed cost), graduate to B |
| IP disputes | Legal friction post-engagement | Define ownership upfront in SOW |
| Over-promise on efficiency | Credibility damage | Conservative estimates (5–10%), let results speak |
